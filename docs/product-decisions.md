# product decisions

Decisions taken during implementation that complement the Figma file. The file and its notes stay the main source; this covers what they leave open or contradict, so it doesn't have to be settled again.

When an open point is decided, move it to the decided list. When a decision changes, edit it here instead of keeping the old version.

## decided

### copy

- **Field errors say what to do about what's there.** Unanswered questions: "escolha uma das opções." (the file has a red-border error state for options but no message; a border alone doesn't say what's wrong). Empty name, or only spaces: "informe como a gente pode te chamar."; something that is clearly not a name: "confira o nome ou apelido que você informou."; over 80 characters: "use até 80 caracteres no nome ou apelido.". Empty WhatsApp: "informe seu WhatsApp com DDD."; letters or symbols that don't belong in a phone number: "use apenas números e sinais de telefone, sem letras ou outros símbolos."; anything else that isn't a number we can use (incomplete, no area code, an area code not in use, too many digits): "confira o DDD e o número do WhatsApp.". All of them sit under their field, in the style of the file's field errors. A valid field gets no message or mark: being well formed doesn't mean the number exists or is the person's.
- **While sending, only the button changes.** It says "enviando..."; screen readers hear "enviando sua resposta...". The states frame shows "a resposta está sendo processada. evite enviar novamente." under the button; it's not shown, since the page itself prevents a second send.
- **Failures say only what is known.** "não conseguimos guardar sua resposta." when the server reports that nothing was written; "não conseguimos confirmar o envio." when there's no answer or the server can't tell (offline, timeout, dropped connection); "muitas tentativas por aqui." after a 429; "não deu pra enviar agora." for anything else. Each comes with its short line of what to do. None mentions codes, services or the database.
- **The failure message sits under the button**, where the person just tapped, before the privacy line. Focus stays on the button, so trying again is one tap or Enter away; screen readers hear the message as an alert. It goes away when the form is sent again, and is announced again if the same failure repeats.
- **The privacy line is "a gente usa seus dados só pra organizar o clube e entrar em contato sobre ele."**, followed by the link "como usamos seus dados", underlined in the same size and color. It fits the space the frame's line had, so nothing below moves at 390 and 1440; at 320 it takes one more line (9 px).
- **The opt-in confirmation also appears on desktop.** The mobile confirmation has "também anotamos seu interesse em testar a experiência digital." (shown only with the opt-in); the desktop frame has no equivalent. It goes under the status line in the right column, with the same box as on mobile.
- **Copy that differs between breakpoints stays different**: "anotado!" on mobile and "anotado." on desktop, "2027 / em construção" vs "clube do livro / 2027", the desktop-only "um convite primeiro. as inscrições ficam pra depois.", and the headline line breaks of each frame.
- **Browser title "clube do livro"; link previews use the headline, the proposal and an image** made from the desktop panel: the purple surface, the light group from the file, the rubric, the headline in its five desktop lines and the epilogue. Nothing in it is new copy.

### behavior

- **Errors appear as soon as they're certain, and go as soon as they're fixed.** The file's note says to validate on submit; waiting that long made people find out about a letter in the number only at the end. Now:
  - while typing, only what typing more can't fix is flagged: letters or symbols in the WhatsApp, more digits than any Brazilian number has, a name over 80 characters. An empty or incomplete value is never an error at this point;
  - leaving a field the person typed in flags whatever is still wrong, including an incomplete number, an area code not in use, a field emptied or a name that is clearly not one. Passing through a field without typing says nothing;
  - on submit, every field is checked, the remaining problems are shown and focus goes to the first one in page order. The answers stay as they are, and the button is never disabled for invalid fields;
  - an error goes away with the keystroke that fixes it. While the person edits a field that shows an error, the message stays as long as the same problem is there; a new problem that isn't certain yet (a number being retyped) waits until the field is left;
  - choices are only questioned on submit, and the message goes as soon as one is picked.

  The error is the first thing read with the field, before its help text, and `aria-invalid` follows what is shown. A message that appears or changes while typing or on leaving a field is also read once through the form's status; on submit, focus on the first problem reads it.
- **Names are taken as people write them.** Any alphabet, accents, hyphens, apostrophes, digits and symbols: "m4yco", "ana_07" and "007" are names. Only two things are refused: nothing but symbols ("...", "?") and a number of 6 digits or more without letters, which is usually a phone or a document typed in the wrong field. Spaces at the ends and repeated spaces are tidied on the server; nothing else is changed.
- **The name has no length cap in the field.** With `maxlength`, a long paste was cut at 80 characters without anyone noticing. The field now keeps everything typed or pasted and shows the limit as soon as it's passed; there's no counter, since almost nobody gets near it.
- **WhatsApp accepts what people actually type**: spaces, parentheses, dots, hyphens and dashes, `+55` (or `(+55)`), `55` or a leading 0. Letters and other symbols are never dropped to make a number fit: "31a98765-4321" or "WhatsApp: (31) 98765-4321" stay as typed and are explained. The area code must exist. Mobile numbers have 9 digits starting with 9; 8-digit landlines (WhatsApp Business) are accepted too. There's no mask: the value is only rewritten as in the placeholder, "(31) 98765-4321", when the field loses focus and the number is valid, so the cursor and backspace behave as usual while typing or editing a pasted number.
- **A number already stored is never overwritten by the form.** Typing a number doesn't prove it's yours, and there's no account to prove it with. Different answers for a stored number wait in `change_requests` until the organizers confirm them through that WhatsApp; the same answers add nothing; a retried submission is recorded once. Accounts or codes sent by WhatsApp would cost more than the problem, and the club will message every number anyway, which is the natural moment to confirm.
- **Nothing tells whether a number is already stored.** The API answer and the confirmation are the same in every case. The confirmation stays true in all of them: the answers were noted (as a row or as a request), and the test invite line appears only when the box was ticked, which is recorded either way.
- **The confirmation survives a reload.** Only "sent" and whether the test invite was ticked are kept in the history entry, never the answers. There's no way back to the form from the confirmation, since none is designed.
- **After a 429 the page waits** the time the server asked for (`Retry-After`), showing the same message if the person tries before that.
- **The test invite is independent.** It starts unchecked, doesn't depend on the club answers and is stored as its own column.
- **Sending needs JavaScript.** Without it the browser posts the form to the API, which refuses it; no answer is ever placed in the URL.

### layout

- **One layer owns the side margin.** The page shell (`.page`, on every page) has the only side margin, `--page-gutter`: 24 px as in the phone frames up to 800 px, then 3% of the window, up to 64 px. The container (`.view`) only limits the width and centers it; sections line up on its edges and components keep only their own inner padding. Before, the wide layout had a 40–70 px padding inside a 1440 px container centered with auto margins, so from 1440 px up both added up (110 px a side at 1520), and on phones every section repeated its own 24 px.
- **Two columns from 1240 px, stacked below.** The editorial panel was composed at 560 px: narrower, the proposal takes a fifth line and the light reaches the epilogue (2.2:1 at 461 px). The form needs about 540 px. 1240 px is the first width where both fit with the margins and the gap. Past that the two columns share the width in the frame's proportion (560 : 650), with a gap of 5% of the window (40 to 80 px), up to a 1600 px container; wider windows only add space at the sides. At 1440 the panel is 593 px and the form 688 px (the frame's 560 and 650); at 1920, 703 and 817.
- **Stacked, the page is one column up to 720 px wide.** The 768 frame centers the 390 px phone column, which left nearly 200 px empty on each side of a tablet; now the column takes the width there is, up to 720 px, and centers past that (tablets in landscape, small laptops). On phones nothing changes.
- **Width of the column and width of the text are separate.** Running text (the proposal, notes, help, consent and privacy lines, the confirmation and the notice) stops at 66 characters; questions at about 24 characters, so they break as in the frames; choices, fields, the button and dividers use the whole column.
- **The desktop panel fits the window's height.** The frame's 940 px panel, 60 px from the top, needs a 1060 px window; most laptops are 720 to 900 px tall, and a fixed panel there was always cut at the top or the bottom. So the panel takes the window's height minus a margin (60 px, less on short windows, down to 24 px), up to 940 px. The headline (60 px down to 42 px) and the space around the text scale with the height between 900 and 640 px. The proposal, the context and the epilogue keep their size, and the light keeps its distance to the text: on a shorter panel it loses some of its lower part rather than moving under the text.
- **The panel sticks only when it fits whole in the window**, checked in the page (`panel.ts`), since its height depends on the text. On windows too short even for its smallest version (below about 600 px tall) it scrolls with the page like the rest: top and bottom are always reachable, and it never scrolls on its own.
- **The desktop confirmation panel takes the same height as the form's**, 780 px at 900, as in its frame, so going from one to the other doesn't jump. "a gente se fala." stays at the bottom; the light keeps its place under the title and, as in the form's panel, loses some of its lower part on shorter windows. The mobile confirmation fills the screen, with "a gente se fala." and the signature at the bottom, as in the 390 × 844 frame.
- **The light masses aren't cut at the column edge.** In the 768 frame the light ends sharply at the edge of the 390 px column (top and right). On the page it continues into the margin, as it does at the edge of a phone screen, and the edge of the screen cuts it: that's the one thing the shell clips. Each light keeps the frame's distance to the right edge of its column or panel, so it stays where it was composed whatever their width.
- **At 320, sizes and the extra space under text come from the 320 frame; some boxes don't.** The frame has artifacts of fixed-size boxes that aren't reproduced: the year sits 22 px away from the right edge, the third option is 98 px tall while the others are 77 px, two-line labels sit off-center in their rows, and the divider runs to the edge of the screen. Here the year aligns right, every option is 77 px with its label centered, and the divider keeps the margins. Everything below the first question sits about 20 px higher than in the frame. Below about 335 px "sim, me chama pra testar também" takes two lines: in the frame it fits on one by running into the margin.
- **The privacy notice is a page of its own** (`/privacidade`) in the form's type scale, with the opening light at the top: a reading column of up to 650 px, inside the same page margins. It can be linked directly in a message. Coming from the form, "voltar pro formulário" returns through history, so the answers already filled in are still there.
- **Two kinds of motion, kept apart.** The page itself moves rarely: when it opens and when the answer is confirmed, then it rests. The light moves all the time, slowly, and nothing depends on it. If it stopped, the page would read the same.
- **The page arrives as a surface first, then what's on it.** On wide screens the panel is there, dark, from the first frame and settles 36 px into place over 1.1 s; its light comes up over 1.6 s, and the text 160 ms after the panel, about 50 ms apart, in 800 ms. The form follows 90 ms later in 700 ms, and takes answers from the first frame. Arrivals use one curve, quick to start and long to land (`cubic-bezier(0.22, 1, 0.36, 1)`), with no bounce or overshoot. The confirmation is a new scene rather than the form transformed: the form goes at once, the panel arrives from below, continuing the page's way up, then its light and text. Field messages and failures only fade in, in under 250 ms.
- **The light is matter under a dark surface.** The file marks the groups as "animável no futuro" without saying how. Each desktop panel has three large, soft fields bigger than the panel and partly outside it: a warm near-black for depth, mostly behind the text, a dominant solar color and a second one close to it, below and to the right of the text (terracotta and coral on the form; amber and deep peach on the confirmation). Over them sits the window light from the file: the source behind the opening, the edge that hides it and its reflection. On phones and on the privacy notice, on paper, there's one peach field where the file had a lilac bounce, the warm source, its threshold and its edge. Lilac and rose are gone: everything stays in the solar family.
- **Each layer drifts on its own.** Position on each axis, size, turn and intensity each have their own cycle, from 11 to 37 s, with sine easing and different starting points, so the light changes place and temperature without visibly coming back to where it started and no two layers move together. Fields travel 14 to 27% of the panel's width and 7 to 16% of its height; the window light a few percent, and only away from the text. On phones movements are smaller and there are fewer layers. Only transform and opacity change; out of view the light stops, and with reduced motion it's the still composition and arrivals are a 200 ms fade.
- **Hover isn't styled.** The file defines default, selected, focus and error states; options and the button only change the cursor.

### accessibility

Where the file falls short of WCAG 2.2 AA, the implementation moves as little as possible:

- **The desktop light sits 16 px lower than in the frame.** There, the bright edge of the light reaches the bottom of "comentar" at about 3:1. Lower, the proposal stays above 5:1 at rest and through the whole cycle; the composition reads the same.
- **The light never comes toward the text.** The window light only dims and moves away from it; the warm fields sit below and to the right, grow away from it and never drift up; the near-black field only darkens what's behind it. The tests check every text over the light at rest and across each layer's cycle.
- **The desktop epilogue is white**, not the frame's `papel-quente`. On panels shorter than the frame's it sits over the warm bounce of the light, where `papel-quente` stays around 4.2:1; white keeps it above 4.5:1 there.
- **Field borders reach 3:1.** The frames use `#e8d8d4` (empty, 1.3:1) and `#c8b9b6` (filled, 1.8:1). They are now `#988c87` and `#887d78`: the same colors mixed with `olue/beige chalk/800`, so they stay warm and the filled state stays a step darker. The error border is `cores/marca/highlight` darkened just enough (`#eb6069`, 3.1:1).
- **The button label is 19 px at every width.** The 320 frame uses 16 px, which against the primary color (3.8:1) would need 4.5:1; at 19 px bold it counts as large text, for which 3:1 is enough. It still fits on one line at 320.
- **The checkbox keeps the file's focus state** (a thicker border). It's subtle, but visible, which is what AA asks.

### data and privacy

- **What is stored and why** is in the README: the answers, the number in E.164, the consent version and dates. No IP, user agent, cookies or analytics.
- **The consent record is the exact text shown**: the sentence next to the button, the privacy line, the test invite and the whole privacy notice, stored once per version in `consent_texts`; each response and change request points to its version. `v1` (the first wording) was never published; `v2` adds the notice. A version can be corrected while nothing points to it; after that, a change means a new version.
- **The privacy notice is short and plain.** Five parts: who organizes, why the form asks for a name and a WhatsApp, how the test invite works, how long answers are kept and how to stop, correct or delete. It covers what is true for this form, without legal boilerplate.
- **Retention follows the club, not a legal deadline**: answers are kept only while the club is being organized for 2027; when people are contacted, whoever doesn't want to go on leaves the list; if the club doesn't happen in 2027, everything is deleted. This is the proposal written in the notice, for the organizers to confirm or adjust.
- **Missing information shows as pending and doesn't stop the build.** Who organizes and the contact channel are marked as visibly pending, never as information. The site can be deployed and reviewed like that; collecting answers waits for both, by connecting the database only after they're filled in.
- **Rate limiting keeps a keyed hash of the IP for at most an hour**, in a separate table never joined with the responses.
- **The responses are read in Neon's console.** No admin page: the list is small, Neon's table view and SQL editor cover reading, exporting, applying changes and deleting, and there's nothing new to secure.
- **Requests are confirmed through the number itself** before acting, and stopping contact means deleting the row (its change requests go with it). Dropping only the test invite is an update of that column. The README covers exports, Neon's restore window and branches.
- **Region: São Paulo** for the function and the database.

### fonts

- **Satoshi is downloaded at build time.** Its license allows self-hosting on our own site but not redistribution through a repository. The build fetches the official files from Fontshare and serves them from our domain; nothing goes to a third party at runtime.

### deploy

- **Deployed on Vercel from `main`.** The project is the organizers'; `vercel.json` fixes how it's built, so the project settings can't change it. No database was connected by this work: that step turns collection on and waits for the notice (README).
- **Link previews use the production domain**, from `VERCEL_PROJECT_PRODUCTION_URL` or `SITE_URL`, even on preview deployments.
